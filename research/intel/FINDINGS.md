## 2026-10-20 — Seed sweep: acute-cardiac, fall, emergency & long-term multiparameter models

**Heart attack (acute MI) from wearable ECG/PPG.** Single-lead deep-learning MI classifiers report
~97% F1 on curated PhysioNet-style data, but **ST-segment sensitivity is poor (8–12%)** and there is
**no prospective wearable validation** for acute MI. Arrhythmia (AF) detection from PPG/single-lead is
far more mature (~95%). PPG foundation models are emerging (AnyPPG, >100k hours).
→ *Honest stance:* do NOT claim acute-MI event detection. Target AF + arrhythmia + structural-HD risk
+ MI *risk*, clinician-in-the-loop.

**Fall detection.** SisFall lab accuracy ~97–98%, but **real-world false alarms are the hard problem**
(>1/hour on real daily-life data; sitting-down-hard is the classic false trigger). Pre-impact detection
(~260 ms lead) demonstrated. Datasets: SisFall, KFall, MobiFall, and FARSEEING (real falls).
→ Report false-alarms/day, validate on real falls, use a confirmation window.

**Emergency SOS design (Apple pattern).** Detect → wrist tap + alarm + alert → **countdown/confirmation
window** → auto-call only after ~1 min immobility → notify emergency contacts with location. False
positives from rapid-motion-then-stop (skiing, rollercoasters). Lesson: confirmation window + immobility
gate + easy user override are essential; neck/waist placement detects falls better than wrist.

**Long-term multiparameter prediction.** Wearable HRV predicts major adverse cardiovascular events in
healthy middle-aged adults; a transformer fusing HRV + sleep + SpO2 + steps predicted post-PCI
inflammation (AUC 0.88); HRV time-series foundation models (zero-shot forecasting) are new. No clean
AF/diabetes-from-continuous-wearable benchmark found yet.
→ Telomy edge: we already collect ring PPG + patch ECG (HRV, arrhythmia) + SpO2 — fuse with the blood
clocks + disease models for a multimodal risk engine no one has at consumer scale.

Sources: bioengineering wearable-ECG primer; AnyPPG (arXiv 2511.01747); PLOS ONE PPG CNN; SisFall
ensemble (ScienceDirect 2025); cross-dataset real-fall study (ScienceDirect); Apple Fall Detection
support docs; Frontiers HRV review 2025; wearable biomarkers DTx review (Bioengineering 2025).

## 2026-10-21 — Theme 3: Aging-clock SOTA (blood / methylation / proteomic / organ / wearable clocks)

- **Predicting chronological age well does not mean predicting mortality well.** The Biolearn "unified framework" (Ying, Moqri, Gladyshev et al., Nature Aging, Nov 2025) evaluated 39 biomarkers on more than 20k people. Age-prediction accuracy did not correlate with mortality-prediction power (R=0.12, P=0.67). Horvath skin&blood had the best age fit (R²=0.88), while GrimAge2 had the strongest mortality association (HR=2.57). This undercuts every rival that markets "MAE of X years", including us at 8.8y. The metric that should headline is the mortality/morbidity C-index. https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-025-00987-y · https://github.com/bio-learn/biolearn (library; backend of ClockBase)
- **Routine-blood clocks are near their ceiling for mortality.** In UK Biobank (n≈306k), a 25-marker elastic-net Cox clock reached C-index 0.778 against PhenoAge's 0.750, using a 60-biomarker candidate pool (Comms Biol 2023; Fight Aging covered it Oct 2025). A Chinese physiological-aging index scored 0.749 against PhenoAge's 0.743 on UKB external validation, with chronological age alone at 0.706. Gains over PhenoAge are about 0.01–0.03 C-index, so blood-only work has small headroom. Adding PhenoAge acceleration to CVD risk moved C-index only from 0.670 to 0.674. https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-023-05456-z · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-024-03769-2 · https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-12495-5
- **Among methylation clocks, the mortality leaders are GrimAge2 and DunedinPACE.** In InCHIANTI (Nature Aging 2026), baseline plus longitudinal change gave C-index 0.808 for GrimAge2 and 0.800 for DunedinPACE. A clock's slope alone, without the baseline value, did not predict mortality. In BASE-II's comparison of 14 consensus biomarkers (Biomarker Research 2026), DunedinPACE was the strongest mortality predictor. Rankings shift between cohorts and between versions of the Biolearn preprint, so treat "best clock" claims cautiously. https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-026-01066-6 · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40364-026-00909-z
- **Proteomic organ clocks are the research frontier.** Nature Aging 2025 built an organismal clock plus 10 organ-specific clocks on UKB Olink Explore 3072. Cross-cohort correlation was r=0.98 in China (n=3,977) and r=0.93 in the USA (n=800). Organ age predicted disease onset and mortality beyond clinical and genetic risk, and brain age was the strongest mortality signal. A bioRxiv preprint (not peer-reviewed) found that single proteins often beat organ clocks for organ-specific disease, so the extra value of a whole organ clock is contested. The consumer cost of Olink-scale assays makes this impractical in India for now. https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-025-01016-8 · https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.09.627624v1.full
- **The Biomarkers of Aging Challenge Phase II (mortality, ~500 people) went to Team Kauai** with a *proteomic* model that beat GrimAge2. Phase I (age prediction) was won at 2.45y error. Phase III (healthspan/multimorbidity) was due to start Q3 2025; I found no results yet. N is tiny, so read this as a signal rather than a definitive ranking. https://www.agingconsortium.org/challenge · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11565782/
- **Wearable clocks now exist at scale. This is the most important finding for Telomy.** Apple and Princeton's **PpgAge** (Nature Comms 2025) used the Apple Heart & Movement Study (n=213,593; >149M participant-days). MAE was about 2.4y in the healthy cohort and about 3.2y in the general cohort. A positive PpgAge gap was associated with heart disease, heart failure and diabetes, and the clock tracked pregnancy. The study is observational, internal to Apple and not independently replicated, and I found no evidence it has shipped as a consumer feature. Follow-on work includes an open benchmark of PPG foundation models for bio-age (arXiv 2603.14030) and an arXiv paper on smartwatch PPG "heart age" pretrained with ECG guidance (2608.01620). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-64275-4 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.14030 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.01620
- **ECG-age has the strongest non-blood outcome evidence.** A systematic review and meta-analysis found that an AI-ECG delta-age of 6–10y or more is consistently associated with adverse outcomes. In a cohort of about 49k cardiovascular and acute-care patients, a gap of +8y or more gave HR 1.45 for 10-year mortality, and saliency analysis pointed to the P-wave. A DXA self-supervised preprint (UKB n=39,310, only 377 deaths) gave HR 1.06 per gap-year. Retinal eyeAge shows a weak independent HR of about 1.03/yr after PhenoAge adjustment. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12795216 · https://academic.oup.com/ehjdh/article/6/6/1204/8272814 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02208
- **Foundation and LLM approaches are arriving.** EHRFormer (Nature Medicine 2025) is a time-series transformer that builds a whole-lifespan blood clock from routine EHR labs, with 5-year disease-incidence AUCs of 0.80–0.90. A bioRxiv preprint, "The End of Aging Clocks" (Mar 2026), trains a reasoning LLM over methylation, proteomic and clinical data. Both are hospital-scale EHR or omics efforts. Neither does wearable fusion. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-04006-w · https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.28.714980v1.full

**Standout opportunities for Telomy:** (1) **Re-headline our clocks on mortality, not MAE.** Biolearn's R=0.12 result means our 8.8y-MAE blood clock is the wrong number to lead with. We already have NHANES linked mortality (10y AUC 0.885). Report Harrell's C for our blood-age *gap* against PhenoAge (UKB reference about 0.75) and against chronological age, and publish it. Almost no consumer rival does this, so honest mortality-calibrated bio-age is a real differentiator. A practical step is to run our NHANES clocks through Biolearn's evaluation harness for a standardized, citable comparison. (2) **Build a ring PPG-age model as our Indian answer to PpgAge.** Apple has shown wearable PPG age at about 2.4–3.2y MAE with disease associations, but it is locked inside Apple's ecosystem and validated on US data only. Pre-train on the open PPG foundation models benchmarked in arXiv 2603.14030 plus Spandan and Multi-site PPG (Theme 2), then fine-tune on our ring data. ## 2026-10-21 — Theme 4: Wearable cardiac models (ECG/PPG for AF, MI, structural heart disease; PPG foundation models)

- **Structural heart disease (SHD) from a single-lead wearable ECG now has its first prospective consumer-device data, but only as a preprint.** WATCH-SHD (medRxiv, Aug 2026) ran a DL model on point-of-care Apple Watch ECGs. The target was a composite of EF<40%, severe left-sided valve disease and severe LVH. For LV systolic dysfunction it reported sensitivity 86.7% and specificity 91.0%, and AI-guided testing cut the number-needed-to-test by more than 60%. The work is not yet peer-reviewed. https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.13.26360395v1.full · Yale's Wearable-Echo-FM (EHJ-Digital Health) pre-trained contrastively on 274k single-lead ECG–echo-report pairs. It matched a fully supervised CNN, and with about 0.5% of the labels it clearly beat a randomly initialised baseline. Caveat: it used lead I extracted from 12-lead ECGs as a proxy and was not tested on real watch recordings. https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.10.25329163.full.pdf · A related follow-on uses echo-report supervision for fine-grained SHD: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09332 (results not verified).
- **Low-EF detection: accuracy falls when moving from clinic to home recordings, and fine-tuning recovers most of it.** In npj Digit Med 2026, a model with internal AUC about 0.88 dropped to 0.82 on Apple Watch ECGs and to 0.75 on a home patch dataset (QOCA). Fine-tuning on home data brought it back to about 0.86. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02919-5 · The original Mayo Apple Watch result (AUC 0.88) had only 16 EF≤40% cases. https://www.tctmd.com/news/ai-accurately-detects-lv-dysfunction-using-single-lead-apple-watch-ecg · Eko's single-lead stethoscope reached AUROC 0.852, sensitivity 77.5% and specificity 78.3% in a large cohort. https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacadv.2025.101619 → **Lesson: a model trained on clinic data will under-perform on our patch unless we fine-tune on our own home recordings.**

## 2026-10-21 — Theme 5: Fall detection + emergency alerting (real-fall datasets, false-alarms/day)

- **Models trained on simulated falls lose about half their accuracy on real falls.** "Beyond Simulated Benchmarks" (arXiv 2608.13197, Aug 2026) estimates that capturing 100 real falls takes about 100,000 monitored days. The first author's summary says a Transformer scored F1 0.98 on simulated falls and about 0.50 on real patient falls. The same summary says simple impact features (peak acceleration, impact abruptness) lost only about 10% F1 in zero-shot transfer, but raised more false alarms. These numbers come from the author's LinkedIn post, not the paper's abstract, so verify them. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13197
- **Best real-fall benchmark result: FARSEEING streaming.** "Watch Your Step" (arXiv 2509.11789) ran 60+ hours of continuous L5 (lower-back) IMU data at 100 Hz, with 145 real falls from 41 people. A cost-sensitive threshold that weights missed falls heavily gave recall 1.00, precision 0.84 and F1 0.91. The search snippets did not show a false-alarm count per day, so read the full paper. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11789 · A time-series-classification paper on FARSEEING reports F1 0.907 and false alarms "as few as one in three days" (about 0.33/day; the wording is ambiguous). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382940726 · An earlier FARSEEING ML baseline reported 0.56 false alarms/hour. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7697900/ · A 2026 MDPI study trained on SisFall and tested on FARSEEING plus 7 days of continuous wear; it cites **about 8 false alarms/day as typical of clinical trials**, which is unacceptable for an auto-dial product. https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/1/162
- **Access caveat:** FARSEEING has more than 300 verified real falls from 94 people with mean age 76, but only a **subset is available on request**. All of the results above are on back-worn sensors, not wrist or ring. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0263224122010429
- **Wrist-worn models are improving on lab data, and there is still no fall-specific IMU foundation model.** A gated CNN for watches (arXiv 2605.20275, May 2026) reached F1 90–93% across 5 datasets, all simulated. ## 2026-10-21 — Theme 6: Long-term disease/mortality prediction (HRV/SpO2/RHR multiparameter, time-series foundation models)

- **The first public head-to-head of wearable foundation models finds no clear winner, and XGBoost is close behind.** OpenMHC (arXiv 2607.16235, NeurIPS 2026 poster) uses data from Stanford's My Heart Counts study: about 60M hours across 19 channels from 11,894 people, with up to 169 linked health and lifestyle labels. It has three tracks: 32 downstream outcomes, imputation, and forecasting. In its *reimplementations*, Google's **LSM-2 has the best average rank**, and **XGBoost on hand-crafted features is the closest competitor** (it wins on demographics). Generic time-series foundation models transfer poorly to the outcome tasks: **Chronos-2 ranks 7th of 8 and falls below the linear baseline.** The dataset is available to qualified researchers, and the code and weights are open. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16235 · leaderboard https://myheartcounts-openmhc.hf.space/ · https://huggingface.co/MyHeartCounts/openmhc-chronos2-fc → **Takeaway: generic TS foundation models (Chronos, Toto) are fine for forecasting but not for risk prediction. Use wearable-specific encoders or strong GBM baselines.**
- **Apple's WBM shows behaviour data and raw PPG are complementary.** WBM (ICML 2025) was trained on 2.5B hours of weekly behavioural summaries from 162k Apple Heart & Movement Study participants. Pregnancy detection reached AUROC 0.864 alone and **0.921 combined with PPG**. For diabetes, WBM scored 0.765 against **0.829 for PPG**, and combining them added nothing (0.828). Sleep-duration R² was 0.59 for WBM against 0.11 for PPG. It beat the baseline on 39 of 47 disease and medication tasks. These are cross-sectional and prevalent-condition labels, not incident outcomes, and a reviewer warned that the pregnancy gains may partly reflect age and sex. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00191
- **Google LSM, LSM-2 and PH-LLM:** LSM was trained on 40M hours of minute-level HR, HRV, EDA, accelerometer, skin temperature and altimeter data from 165k Fitbit/Pixel users. **LSM-2's Adaptive and Inherited Masking learns from gappy data without imputation**, which suits ring and patch wear patterns that have real gaps. PH-LLM (Nat Med 2025) is a coaching LLM that scored 79% and 88% on sleep and fitness exam questions, but Google's own follow-up (PHIA) found it **could not answer objective queries over raw tabular sensor data**. Tool or agent grounding is still needed. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.13638 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.05321 · https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03888-0 · https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67922-y
- **This theme's best hard-endpoint result is TRUE-HF (Nature Medicine 2026).** A deep model estimated **daily peak VO2 from Apple Watch data** (r=0.85 against cardiopulmonary exercise testing) in 217 patients with heart failure. **Each 10% drop gave HR 3.62 (95% CI 1.37–9.55) for unplanned admission or IV diuretics, with a median lead time of 7.4 days.** External validation in All of Us, using a reduced-sensor cross-platform model, was weaker: HR 1.32 (1.03–1.69), 21-day lead time. The sample is small, follow-up was about 3 months, and the hazard ratios have wide confidence intervals, but it is a *prospective* design with an external cohort. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04247-3 · https://www.cfrjournal.com/news/smartwatch-data-predicts-heart-failure-exacerbations
- **Longer averaging windows find more disease associations.** A medRxiv preprint (Fulda et al., Jan 2026) analysed 11M Fitbit days from 29,351 All of Us participants against more than 700 incident and 1,300 prevalent EHR outcomes. **One-year step count was associated with 37 incident and 373 prevalent outcomes after Bonferroni correction; a one-day window gave 17 and 231.** The usual practice of summarising over 7 days throws signal away. The preprint reports associations only, not C-indices. https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.29.26344899v1 · PMC12870592
- **Single-signal mortality evidence is solid but modest.** For HRV, the 2022 IPD meta-analysis (Jarczok et al., 32 studies, n≈38k) found lower HRV predicts all-cause and cardiac mortality across ages, sexes and recording lengths, with similar hazard ratios across HRV parameters; I could not retrieve the pooled HR in this pass. In CV patients the pooled HR is 2.12 for all-cause death (2020). https://doaj.org/article/c9bb1f5b82574c8d9d250b9250ebbf46 · For resting heart rate in UK Biobank, men have about 22% higher all-cause mortality per +10 bpm. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7259773/ · For motion-only data, UKB accelerometry plus age and sex gives a **5-year mortality C-index of 0.72**. https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pdig.0000045 · A small prospective smartwatch study of hypertension onset (n=230) gave AUC 0.67 for smartwatch plus clinical data against 0.54 for clinical data alone. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12872-026-06073-4
- **SpO2 gap: no wearable SpO2 cohort with hard endpoints found.** Mortality evidence for nocturnal hypoxaemic burden (T90 or desaturation burden) still comes from **PSG and oximetry cohorts**. In type 2 diabetes, top-quartile T90 gave MACE HR 1.48; a community sleep-apnoea cohort with 11-year follow-up (341 CV deaths) links event-weighted desaturation burden to CV mortality. The best older signal was 80% of infections flagged at a median 3 days before symptoms (n=3,318, pandemic-era). I found no 2025–26 prospective ring study reporting both sensitivity and lead time. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12140236/ · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8799466/ → Treat "illness-onset" alerts as low-confidence nudges, not alarms.

## 2026-10-22 — Theme 9: Exposome & lifestyle (environmental exposures, air quality, diet, microbiome → aging)

- **In UK Biobank, exposures outweigh genes for mortality.** Argentieri et al. (*Nature Medicine*, Feb 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-03483-9) screened 164 environmental, social and behavioural factors and kept 85 mortality-associated exposures after confounding checks. Of those, **25 independent exposures were also linked to a proteomic age clock** (n≈45k). The main ones were smoking, physical activity, financial and socio-economic status, and living situation. **Together these 25 explained more mortality variance than polygenic risk did.** On average each exposure was tied to about 22 aging biomarkers and 15 age-related diseases. The data are observational: the authors flag residual confounding and say the study does not establish causation. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922759/ · https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-025-00845-x · https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-study-looking-at-genetic-and-lifestyle-factors-and-premature-death-ageing-and-age-related-diseases/
- **Heat ages people measurably (Science Advances, Feb 2025).** Choi & Ailshire studied 3,686 US adults aged 56+ (HRS). More extreme-heat days in a person's neighbourhood went with faster epigenetic aging, **up to about 14 months more biological aging** in high-heat versus low-heat areas. PCPhenoAge responded within 7–60 days; GrimAge and DunedinPACE only over longer windows. This is observational, US-only, and limited to people aged 56+. **There is no Indian equivalent**, even though India is among the regions most exposed to heat stress worldwide. Wearable heat-stress evidence from India is thin: only 3 studies (about 5.7%) in one scoping review of climate-extreme wearable studies were Indian, and consumer devices lack core temperature. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adr0616 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12569015/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9508665/
- **Air pollution → epigenetic aging: the evidence is positive in Asia but inconclusive overall.** In the Taiwan Biobank (n=2,462, 2026), PM2.5 and PM10 accelerated all three clocks tested. A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis calls long-term PM2.5 effects on Horvath age "possible but inconclusive", and a US cohort of 4,018 found **no** association across five clocks, including GrimAge and DunedinPACE. **I found no Indian cohort linking PM2.5 to DNA methylation or biological age.** The Indian evidence is cognitive: **LASI-DAD (two waves) links long-term PM2.5 to faster cognitive decline, with residential solid-fuel combustion the most harmful source**, and about 48% of LASI's ~61.7k adults aged 45+ use highly polluting cooking fuel. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41412051/ · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S014765132600093X · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12906657/ · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12877-024-04662-6
- **What is actually modifiable: the effects are metabolic first and lifestyle second, and smaller than the hype.** The MEAB-Index meta-analysis (IJMS, Jun 2026; 60 studies) pooled **about +0.31 years of epigenetic age acceleration per unit of adverse exposure**, strongest for metabolic and inflammatory factors. The pooled effects for **diet (12 studies, about −0.06) and physical activity (7 studies, about −0.28) both crossed zero, with I² of about 95–99%.** This is an important correction to marketing claims that diet reverses biological age. By contrast, **Life's Essential 8 (LE8)** is consistently linked to PhenoAge in cross-sectional NHANES data: **each +10 LE8 points goes with about 1.2 fewer years of PhenoAge acceleration** (Sci Rep 2025; OR 0.65 for accelerated aging). The LE8 + PhenoAge combination best predicted 15-year CVD mortality in UK Biobank prediabetics. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13256709/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12365275 · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40001-025-03218-7
- **Wearable-measurable lifestyle signals with hard outcomes.** In UK Biobank, **sleep regularity (SRI)** from 7 days of wrist accelerometry in 88,975 people was non-linearly linked to mortality: the 5th percentile had **HR 1.53** against the median over 7.1 years. - **Microbiome clocks are weak, and nothing Indian exists.** Gut-microbiome age models reach an **MAE of about 6–9.5 years**: about 9.5 years on 78.6k stools with prospective validation, 5.9 years for a small DNN validation set, and 8.6 years for a Chinese multi-view ensemble. They are far worse than blood clocks. A 2026 *Nature Communications* **oral-microbiome clock** (NHANES n=4,675 plus an external cohort of 1,293) is a newer direction, and saliva is easier to collect than stool. India's open data are dated: **LogMPIE** (2018, 16S only, n=1,004, 14 sites; ENA PRJEB25642, though one listing gives a conflicting accession) is dominated by *Prevotella copri*, a profile unlike Western cohorts. **Verdict: microbiome is not worth building a clock on yet.** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2589004223026159 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7298543 · https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-72096-2 · https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata2018232

## 2026-10-22 — Theme 10: Nutrition & food recognition (Indian recipe/portion datasets, food-image SOTA)

- **Recognising Indian dishes is close to solved. Estimating portion size is not.** **Khana** (arXiv 2509.06006, Sep 2025) has about 131k images across 80 Indian dish classes at 500×500, split 70/15/15. **The best published baseline is ConvNeXt-S at 86.7% top-1 and 97.6% top-5**, with ViT-B/16 at 85.3%. The classes are imbalanced and the paper gives no weight or nutrient labels. Older Indian sets (Kaggle "Indian Food Images" 80 classes, IndianFood101-type sets) report about 80–93% accuracy. These are retrospective accuracy figures on curated web images, not real plates. **I found no public Indian-food dataset with weighed portions or a thali-level ground truth.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06006 · https://khana.omkarprabhu.in/ · https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/iamsouravbanerjee/indian-food-images-dataset · https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1753764/full
- **Off-the-shelf VLMs are now strong at estimating calories on Western plates.** A 2026 bioRxiv benchmark tested ten VLMs on Nutrition5k. **Gemini 3.0 Flash scored best (CCC 0.767, calorie MAE 80.7 kcal per plate). Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite came close (CCC 0.754) at about $0.59 per 1k images, and Gemini 2.0 Flash reached CCC 0.742 at about $0.10 per 1k.** The authors also re-checked 440 images by hand and found **Nutrition5k labels systematically omit ingredients**: after correction, ingredient-overlap Jaccard rose from 0.62 to about 0.82. An earlier 2025 benchmark put GPT-4o at about 166 kcal MAE, improved by about 12 kcal when location or meal metadata was added. Food-R1 (2026, an RL-tuned food VLM) claims about half the error of GPT-4o-mini, but I could not verify which metric that refers to. **All of this is on Nutrition5k cafeteria plates, a US setting. None of it covers Indian mixed dishes, gravies or oil.** https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.26.740845v1 · https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07048v1 · https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04986v1
- **Accurate on average, wrong for individuals: the important caveat.** A 2026 feeding-trial paper on general-purpose LLMs found that **average energy-intake error is small but per-meal and per-person errors are wide**. DietAI24 (Communications Medicine 2025) instead grounds an MLLM in a nutrient database via RAG and **reports 63% lower MAE on real-world mixed dishes** than plain GPT Vision. The pattern that works is: VLM to identify the dish, then database lookup, then a separate portion estimate. This matches our tool-grounded LLX design (theme 7). https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26538 · https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-025-01159-0
- **Portion estimation SOTA is moving to monocular, depth-free geometry.** Adding a geometry head to an MLLM cut per-food portion error by **33–41%** (arXiv 2607.16514), and it skips depth sensors because phones rarely provide them. Real-scale 3D reconstruction from a single image (arXiv 2601.20051) and **Open-KNEAD** (agentic decomposition on a Qwen3.5 backbone with UniDepth metric depth, arXiv 2607.12911) point the same way. The labelled portion benchmarks are **FPB** (IS2AI, Hugging Face; weighed items in S/M/L sizes, multi-angle), Nutrition5k and OmniFood8K (weighed ingredients). **Whether FPB and OmniFood8K contain any Indian dishes is unconfirmed.** I did not verify open-weights status for these models. https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16514v1 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12911 · https://huggingface.co/datasets/issai/Food_Portion_Benchmark · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.12356
- **The Indian nutrient backbone is open and usable.** The **Indian Nutrient Databank (INDB)**, built on IFCT-2017 (Jaacks et al., 2024), has **1,095 raw foods and 1,014 cooked recipes**, with nutrients per 100 g and per serving, Excel files and Stata code on GitHub. The gaps were filled from IFCT-2004, the UK database (144 foods) and USDA (54 foods). The authors warn that **composite-dish nutrients vary widely** depending on the recipe, especially oil, and that IFCT-2017 itself covers only raw foods. **FKG.in** (an Indian food knowledge graph) uses LLMs to estimate recipe nutrition from text, but its weight estimates still need a human. https://github.com/lindsayjaacks/Indian-Nutrient-Databank-INDB- · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11277795/ · https://github.com/ifct2017/ifct2017 · https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05248
- **Incumbents' accuracy claims are weak or unvalidated.** HealthifyMe Snap claims about 150k Indian items but a **60–70% recognition rate**, a launch-era figure with no published method, and relies on human reviewers. The only peer-reviewed multi-app comparison (Nutrients 2024, Univ. of Sydney) scored food-*identification* only and found portion size the weak point. Passio (3.5M foods, SDK and REST, token pricing, no free trial) does not document Indian coverage. https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/16/15/2573 · https://robots.net/news/new-feature-introduces-ai-powered-image-recognition-for-indian-food-tracking/ · https://www.passio.ai/platform
- **Glycaemic personalisation in India is still pre-evidence.** A **14-site Indian cohort of people with T2D on Libre CGM with standardised meals** (JMIR protocol, PMC11803329) aims to build ML models of individual postprandial glucose response. **I did not find results published yet.** Separately, **CGMs overestimate post-meal glycaemia, with person-specific bias** (AJCN 2025), which undercuts consumer "food score" apps. I found no peer-reviewed validation of Sugar.fit, Ultrahuman M1 or Fitterfly food scoring. **On regulation:** ICMR-NIN *Dietary Guidelines for Indians* (May 2024) define HFSS and ultra-processed foods, and FSSAI said (June 2026, per ThePrint) it will adopt the ICMR-NIN HFSS thresholds for mandatory front-of-pack labels under Supreme Court direction. **I could not confirm the final rule.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11803329/ · https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165(25)00092-9/fulltext · https://theprint.in/health/fssai-to-adopt-icmr-nutrition-norms-for-defining-food-high-in-fat-sugar-salt-on-front-of-pack-labels/2657660/

**Standout opportunities for Telomy:** (1) **Build the first weighed Indian-plate benchmark, "Telomy-Thali".** Collect a few thousand phone photos of home and canteen Indian meals with a kitchen scale and per-component weights, map them to INDB recipe IDs and add an oil/ghee field. Nobody has this: Khana has no weights, and Nutrition5k and FPB are not Indian. It would let us publish honest calorie-MAE figures where HealthifyMe only quotes about 60–70% recognition. It is a data moat and a paper. (2) **Ship a grounded pipeline rather than training a vision model from scratch.** Use a cheap VLM (Gemini Flash-Lite class, about $0.6 per 1k images, or an open Qwen-VL for on-device or privacy) to identify the dish, look it up in the INDB/IFCT recipe database (as in DietAI24), then run a separate portion step (a monocular geometry or depth head, a reference object like a thali or katori, plus a one-tap S/M/L confirmation). Show **ranges, not point calories**, because the per-meal error is wide. This plugs straight into LLX tool-calling and the telomy-vision project. (3) **Link food to biology, which the calorie apps cannot do.** Feed logged meals into the clocks and levers screen: dietary protein and ALMI in the muscle clock, HFSS/UPF share using the ICMR-NIN definitions (the same basis FSSAI is adopting, so it is regulator-aligned), and post-meal HR/HRV from the ring. Once the Indian PPGR cohort publishes, add CGM-optional glycaemic-response modelling, with an explicit warning about CGM bias. (4) **Don't over-claim.** Even top VLMs reach only about 80 kcal MAE on easy Western plates, and diet → biological-age effects are small and heterogeneous (theme 9). Market this as "awareness and patterns", not "diet reverses your age", consistent with wellness-claim discipline (theme 8).

## 2026-10-22 — Theme 11: Genomics (Indian population genomics, pharmacogenomics, polygenic risk scores)

- **GenomeIndia is published, and the data exists at scale, but it is only available through managed access.** Nature Genetics 57:767–773 (2025) reports **9,768–9,772 healthy, unrelated individuals from 83 populations**, about 130M variants, of which roughly a third (about 44M per press) were previously unreported. IBDC holds **FASTQ (about 700 TB), gVCFs for 9,772 samples (about 35 TB), joint-call files (about 3.5 TB) and phenotype data for 9,330 people**. Access goes through the **IBDC Data Access Portal under the FeED protocol and Biotech-PRIDE guidelines**, which have open, managed and no-access tiers. DBT says independent requests are accepted outside the formal "Call". **I found no statement on whether startups or commercial users are eligible**, so treat that as unknown until we apply. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02153-x · https://ibdc.dbt.gov.in/img/feed_protocol.pdf · https://ibdc.dbt.gov.in/data_access/ · https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2125504
- **Pharmacogenomics (PGx) is the most actionable genomic layer for Indians.** In 1,029 IndiGen genomes (Sahana et al., CTS 2022), **an average Indian carries about 8 PGx variants that directly change drug choice or dose**, and there are 134 common potentially deleterious PGx variants across 102 pharmacogenes. A GenomeIndia-scale "Atlas of Indian Genetic Diversity" (ResearchGate preprint, **not peer-reviewed and only partly verified**) reports 31 of 34 Very Important Pharmacogenes covered and a **median of 4 actionable variants per person**. A 2025 scoping review lists the actionable PGx variants relevant to Indian clinical practice. https://ascpt.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cts.13153 · https://doi.org/10.3390/jox15040101 · https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403107592_An_Atlas_of_Indian_Genetic_Diversity
- **CYP2C19 and clopidogrel is the strongest single case.** The Indian **CYP2C19\*2 allele frequency is about 40%** (versus about 15% in Europeans), and about 31% of Indians are \*1/\*2. In a South Indian clopidogrel-treated CAD cohort (Sci Rep 2025), **68.5% were intermediate or poor metabolizers**. That is a patient cohort, so it is not a population prevalence figure. The CPIC evidence base included **no South or Southeast Asian studies**, so the guidelines are extrapolated to Indians. Clopidogrel is very widely prescribed in India, which makes this a large and under-served clinical gap. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-96338-3 · https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1572886/full · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37602077/
- **Polygenic risk scores (PRS) work moderately in South Asians, but almost all validation is in the diaspora (UK), not in India.** For CAD: GPS_CAD (JACC 2020) gives an AUC of about 0.80 *with age, sex and PCs*. The top 5% has an OR of about 3.2 in an Indian study (versus 4.2 in UKB South Asians). GPS_Mult (Nat Med 2023) gives an OR of 1.83 per SD in 16,874 Genes & Health South Asians. PRS-CSx multi-ancestry gives an OR of 2.67 per SD and an AUC of 0.80 in UKB South Asians (517 cases). For T2D: an Indian PRS (UKB Indians, 959 cases) gives an AUC of 0.795 and 0.778 in **only 445 Indians**, with the top quartile at 5.8× risk. **Caveat: most of these AUCs come from the clinical covariates. The increment from PRS alone is small, and I found no prospective Indian validation from 2025–26.** https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2020.06.024 · https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02429-x · https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCGEN.123.004272 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10354074/
- **Monogenic cardiometabolic disease in India is under-diagnosed and has no national prevalence estimate.** For FH, the conservative estimate is 1:1000 (about 1.3M heterozygotes), and FH is found in about 15% of premature-CAD patients in North India. Small 2025–26 NGS cohorts (Telangana n=30; South India n=116, with 13 pathogenic variants and 26 VUS) show **many VUS and novel LDLR variants**, which is a problem for interpretation. Lp(a): **about 25% of Indians are said to have elevated Lp(a)**, but this is a Novartis-linked press figure that needs primary verification. Lp(a) is genetically determined, a one-time test, and rarely ordered. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12794523/ · https://openaccess.sgul.ac.uk/id/eprint/118513 · https://biospectrumindia.com/news/16/26729/hidden-genetic-threat-putting-1-in-4-at-cardiovascular-disease-risk-in-india-novartis-study.html
- **Low-pass WGS plus imputation beats arrays for PRS, but there is no public Indian imputation panel.** Low-pass sequencing gives lower PRS measurement error than arrays (Li et al., Genome Res 2021), and a 2025 bioRxiv paper benchmarks low-coverage WGS imputation and PRS across five superpopulations, including South Asian. Imputation quality depends on how well the reference panel represents the population. **GenomeIndia gVCFs could form the first Indian panel, but no hosted one exists** (unverified: there may be internal IBDC work). https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.04.29.068452.full.pdf · https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.18.665609v1.full · https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.24.660721v2.full
- **Market and regulation: Indian consumer DNA is a crowded, low-trust, wellness-claim market.** Mapmygenome Genomepatri costs ₹7,999–17,000 (prices conflict between sources) and its WGS ₹89,000. Helixline Decode costs ₹12,999. Xcode Life sells ₹700–2,800 reports on uploaded raw data. MedGenome and Strand sell clinical rather than consumer tests (no pricing found). **There is no DTC-specific regulation and no genetic non-discrimination law (no GINA equivalent).** The ICMR 2022 genetic-testing guidelines require written consent and pre- and post-test counselling. DPDP Rules 2025 apply, but the Act has no separate genetic-data category. Global cautionary case: **23andMe went bankrupt and was sold to Wojcicki's nonprofit TTAM for $305M (closed July 2025), outbidding Regeneron's $256M.** Consumer trust in genetic data is now a selling point in its own right. https://mapmygenome.in/blogs/learn/how-much-does-a-dna-test-cost-in-india · https://helixline.in/blog/dna-test-cost-india-2026 · https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/01/05/genetic-data-protection-dpdpa-india-compliance-analysis/ · https://www.genomeweb.com/business-news/bankruptcy-court-approves-ttam-research-institute-acquisition-23andme

## 2026-10-27 — Re-sweep Theme 5: Fall detection + emergency alerting — what changed since 21 Oct

- **Correction to my 21-Oct note, from the actual abstract of "Beyond Simulated Benchmarks" (arXiv 2608.13197, submitted 13 Aug 2026).** The direction holds, and the important point is *which* models fail. The paper compares interval-, kernel-, symbolic- and **foundation-model** representations on FallAllD (simulated) vs FARSEEING (real). "Highly parameterized kernel and foundation models" do well on simulated data and **degrade the most under scarcity and domain shift**. **Interval-based features give the best absolute real-world performance.** Symbolic sequences with physical impact descriptors degrade least and keep sensitivity, at lower precision. The abstract has no numbers, so the F1 0.98→0.50 I logged on 21 Oct is still LinkedIn-sourced and unverified. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13197
- **FARSEEING size depends on who you ask.** "Watch Your Step" describes **208 clinically verified falls from 92 participants**, and analyses 145 falls from 41 participants at the L5 site. "Beyond Simulated Benchmarks" uses 150 falls from 54 participants (L5, 100 Hz). Our 21-Oct ">300 falls / 94 people" came from an older secondary source. Sensors are L5 or thigh, at 20 or 100 Hz. **I again found no wrist, finger or chest-patch evaluation on FARSEEING.** The closest is SmartFall, which resampled trunk data to 31.25 Hz to test a watch model and itself flags the mismatch. Non-trunk real-fall evidence is still an open gap. https://arxiv.org/html/2509.11789 · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6210545/
- **"99.99% specificity" still means many false alerts, because falls are rare.** These primary studies were collated by aengram, an Australian ambient-sensing vendor, so check the originals: **Scheurer 2019 (Sensors, AIDE-MOI)** saw 12 false flags in 735,852 non-fall minutes (specificity 99.9984%), yet **12 of 28 alerts (43%) were false**, and 4 falls were missed. **Chaudhuri 2015 (JAGS)**, a commercial wearable worn by community-dwelling older adults, gave **84 alarms, 1 true and 83 false**. It detected 1 of 4 falls while worn, and 8 more falls happened while the device was *not worn*. **Bagalà 2012 (PLoS ONE)** tested 13 algorithms on 29 real falls: mean sensitivity 57% (best 82.8%), and **3–85 false alarms per 24 h**. **Aziz 2017** reported 0.05 FA/h in long-term care and 0.15/h in PSP. The aengram piece recommends reporting an **"alert ledger"** (monitored hours, alerts, confirmed, missed, uncertain) instead of sensitivity and specificity. https://aengram.ai/articles/the-arithmetic-of-a-false-alarm · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0263224124008777

**Standout opportunities for Telomy:** (1) **Copy WEAR-ME, but for India and with incident outcomes.** Google has shown that *wearable embedding + routine blood* beats blood alone for insulin resistance (0.76→0.88), and we already hold both halves: the NHANES blood clock and ring/patch/WHOOP streams. Build a "Telomy metabolic risk" model that fuses a ₹600 CBC+CMP+lipid+FBG panel with 2–4 weeks of ring HR/HRV/steps/sleep. Validate it against HOMA-IR (fasting insulin is a cheap add-on at partner labs) in Indian users, who carry a higher IR burden at lower BMI. WEAR-ME has no South Asian validation, so this is a defensible India-first claim. The honest framing is "IR screening", not diabetes prediction. (2) **Make SleepFM-on-wearables our research bet.** SleepFM's 0.84 mortality C-index comes from lab PSG, and nobody has shown how much survives on consumer wearables. TelomyRec already records patch ECG + ring PPG/SpO2 + oximeter + WHOOP on one clock, which is a "mini-PSG". Distil SleepFM embeddings into a ring+patch student model, using nights where we also have home-sleep-test or PSG data. Even reporting "what fraction of SleepFM's signal a ring retains" would be a first, and it would close the wearable-SpO2-outcome gap from Day 6. (3) **Position wearable risk where the increment is real: the already-sick.** The UKB evidence shows +0.008 C in the general population against +0.028 in those with prior disease. So headline "risk of deterioration" in the clinician workspace (HF, DM+HTN, post-cardiac patients) rather than a consumer "death clock". For DM+HTN patients, track **peak-30-minute intensity**, which beats daily volume for mortality, as a named coached metric. (4) **Use cheap, honest engines, and keep forecasting separate from risk.** For risk, a frozen motion/PPG encoder (Inertia-1 or LSM-style) + survival head + XGBoost baseline. For "next 2h HRV/HR" nudges, zero-shot TimesFM or Chronos. A steps-only fallback model (StepFM-style) lets phone-only users get a score before they buy a ring, which turns it into a funnel. Never present single-metric RHR as an HF alarm, since the 2026 RCT shows it doesn't stand alone.
